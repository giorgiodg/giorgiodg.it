Does this sound too 2000-ish? Let me explain... 

## RSS feeds back in the days

In the early 2000's RSS feeds emerged as the _de facto standard_ to publish and consume internet content, mostly coming from blogs.
Back in the day of the <a href="https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Blogosphere" target="_blank">blogosphere</a>, keeping up to date meant spending time on your feed reader. An activity that we might perhaps describe as the forerunner of today's scrolling, why not?  
Although the underlying technology could look like something for geeks, some applications like Google Reader (web) or Liferea and Akregator (Linux) lowered the usability barrier. And when Big G, with its usual ruthlessness, decided to shutdown Reader in 2013, many mourning users quickly looked for alternatives. So much so that good alternatives like Feedly faced a huge [signup increase](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Google_Reader#Discontinuation) in the following days.  
Although the Google Reader alternatives were pretty good, the rise of social media made the critical mass migrate to these platforms, therefore RSS feeds lost their momentum from that point onwards.

## RSS feeds today

Unfortunately, compared to the past, many websites no longer offer their content as RSS feeds, or this is not made explicit.  
Sometimes you need to inspect the page source code to find the proper URL to add it to the reader.  
Fortunately, the wide use of WordPress has helped to keep the availability of RSS feeds fairly high, as WordPress generates them by default. This makes it very convenient to follow websites built with WordPress via RSS.  
However, some authors have ditched WordPress in favour of other solutions, such as static websites built with [Astro](/welcome-to-my-new-website), Jekyll or Eleventy which generally don't enable RSS feeds by default.

## So why talk about it in 2026?  

I've always considered RSS feeds a very convenient way to carefully pick and organise the sources I want to follow, and to read them at my own pace.  
This is hard to achieve via any social media, where every time you open the page or the app, you can't pick up from where you left before. Because you're shown new content suggested by the algorithm.  
Another advantage of RSS feeds is that you can consult them using a wide variety of readers without having to create an account or download a specific app. Unlike social media platforms, which always require an account and collect a vast amount of personal data.  
And it's just that part of becoming less reliant on platform lock-in which led me to this. 
Creating less and less content on social media is a choice I believe is necessary in today's world, in the view of making the web a little bit more decentralised and less dependent on a handful of players. Especially when the terms and conditions change irrevocably, as is the case with the training of AI models.

So, if you've read this far and you have a website, please consider enabling RSS feeds. And if you want to read more posts from me, just paste this website URL into your feed reader and it will do the rest.
